Ramadan meal plan: easy suhoor and iftar ideas for 30 days

Updated 2026-11-06

In Ramadan the kitchen can take over the day: long shopping lists, cooking before iftar, washing up late at night and waking again for suhoor. A simple meal plan gives you that time back for prayer, Quran and family.

This guide shows a calm way to plan the month: a few reliable suhoor ideas, an iftar formula, a weekly rotation you can repeat, and shopping and preparation tips. Adjust everything to your family’s taste, budget and any dietary advice you have been given.

Start with a simple formula, not 30 different recipes

The biggest mistake is planning a new dish every night. Instead, use a formula and change only one part each day.

  • Iftar: dates and water, a soup, one main dish, a salad or vegetables, and fruit.
  • Suhoor: something slow and filling, a protein, and plenty of water.

With a formula, planning the week takes ten minutes and the shopping list almost writes itself.

Easy suhoor ideas

Choose meals that are quick to make half-asleep and keep you comfortable until iftar:

  • Oats cooked with milk, topped with banana and a spoon of nut butter.
  • Eggs (boiled, scrambled or an omelette) with wholemeal bread and cucumber.
  • Yoghurt with fruit, seeds and a little honey.
  • Leftover soup or lentils from iftar, warmed up.
  • Cheese, olives, tomatoes and bread — a classic that needs no cooking.

Many people find that very salty or very sugary food at suhoor makes them thirsty during the day, so keep those for iftar. Drink water steadily between iftar and suhoor rather than all at once.

Iftar ideas that rotate easily

Pick a soup and a main for each day of the week, then simply repeat the week with small changes:

  • Soups: lentil soup, harira, vegetable soup, chicken and vermicelli soup.
  • Mains: baked chicken with potatoes, a vegetable tagine, rice with meat or chickpeas, pasta bake, fish with salad, stuffed vegetables, and one “leftovers” night.
  • Sides: a big salad, bread or msemen, and one small sweet for the family.

Breaking the fast with dates and water, then praying Maghrib before the main meal, also makes iftar lighter and calmer.

A sample weekly rotation

  1. Day 1: lentil soup · baked chicken and potatoes · salad
  2. Day 2: harira · vegetable tagine · bread
  3. Day 3: vegetable soup · rice with chickpeas · yoghurt
  4. Day 4: chicken soup · pasta bake · salad
  5. Day 5: harira · fish with roasted vegetables
  6. Day 6: lentil soup · stuffed peppers or courgettes
  7. Day 7: leftovers night or a simple shared meal with family

Write the rotation on one page and repeat it four times. Swap single dishes when you invite guests or want variety.

Shop and prepare once a week

Make one shopping list from the rotation and buy dry foods (lentils, rice, pasta, oats, dates, spices) for the whole month before Ramadan begins. Then one short preparation session a week saves a lot of evening stress:

  • Wash and chop onions, herbs and vegetables and keep them in boxes.
  • Cook a big pot of soup and freeze portions.
  • Marinate meat or chicken for two days ahead.
  • Prepare a jar of salad dressing.

Sharing the jobs helps too: children can set the table, prepare dates and water, or wash fruit.

Plan the month on paper

When the plan is written down, nobody asks “what are we eating?” at 5 pm. The Ramadan Planner 2027 includes four weekly suhoor and iftar meal plans, a grocery list, a Ramadan and Eid budget, and daily pages for prayers, Quran and reflection — so food, worship and spending are planned in one place.

Above all, keep it simple. A modest table shared with family and neighbours is part of the beauty of the month.

Questions people ask

What should I eat at suhoor to stay full?

Many people choose slow foods such as oats, eggs, yoghurt, wholemeal bread and beans, with plenty of water. If you have a health condition, follow the advice of your doctor.

How can I save money on Ramadan food?

Plan the week, buy dry foods in bulk before Ramadan, cook big pots of soup, use leftovers, and limit fried food and sweets to a few nights a week.

How many dishes do I need for iftar?

There is no rule. A soup, one main dish and a salad is plenty for most families, especially when meals rotate through the week.
